    How a Core Talk round works

    Each round takes about fifteen minutes and trains the skill most people skip: explaining an unfamiliar idea out loud, clearly and in your own words.

    Draw a topic

    Pick the fields you care about and draw a random topic you haven't spoken on yet, plus a speaking twist to keep it interesting.

    Research it

    Use the research timer to read up on the topic and note down three points. Short, focused research beats long, unfocused reading.

    Speak and record

    When time starts, talk about the topic without notes. The app records you until your speaking time runs out.

    Get your score

    Core Talk rates your talk out of 100 and writes a short review of what worked and what to fix. Play your recording back to hear it for yourself.

    A simple shape for a one-minute talk

    If you freeze, use this structure. It works for any topic, from a UPSC concept to a machine you've never seen.

    Open (10 seconds)

    Say what the topic is in one plain sentence, or start with a surprising fact or a real-life example.

    Explain (40 seconds)

    Cover your three points. For each one, give a reason, a number, or an example so it sticks.

    Close (10 seconds)

    Finish with one sentence on why it matters. A clear ending makes you sound confident.

    Why practise this way

    Reading about a topic feels like understanding it, but speaking shows what you actually know. When you have to explain something aloud within a minute, the gaps appear immediately. Hearing your own recording is uncomfortable at first and very useful, because you notice filler words, long pauses, and unclear sentences that you'd never catch while talking. Practising on a wide range of topics also trains you for interviews, group discussions, presentations, and viva exams, where you rarely get to choose the subject.

    Questions people ask

    Is Core Talk really free?

    Yes. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to pay.

    How is my score calculated?

    Out of 100: pace and length (20), fluency, meaning few filler and repeated words (20), pauses (15), depth, meaning examples, reasons and specific numbers or names (20), topic coverage, meaning whether you used the topic's key terms and precise vocabulary (15), and structure, meaning an opening, signposting and a close (10). Pace and pauses come from your voice, and the rest comes from the transcript. The score cannot check whether your facts are correct, so treat it as feedback on how you communicate. Speech recognition can miss technical words and often drops filler sounds like 'um', so real fillers may be undercounted.

    Where are my recordings stored?

    Only in your own browser, on your own device. Core Talk has no server and never receives your recordings. You can delete one recording or clear everything from the app. One thing to know: to score your talk, Chrome and Edge convert your speech to text using their maker's online speech service, so that audio is handled under Google's or Microsoft's privacy terms. Firefox and Safari don't support this, so there you get only a partial score.

    Why can't I see my old recordings on another phone or browser?

    Because they never leave the device where you recorded them. Each browser keeps its own list. Clearing your browser data will also remove them.

    How can I get the most precise score?

    Record in a quiet place, hold your phone or microphone at a steady distance, and speak clearly at a normal pace. Background noise, music or other voices can hide your words from speech recognition and make your pauses harder to measure, which lowers the accuracy of your score.

    The microphone isn't working. What should I do?

    Allow microphone access when your browser asks. If you blocked it earlier, open the site settings in your browser, allow the microphone for this page, and reload.

    Can I change how long I research and speak?

    Yes. Use the timer boxes at the top. The default is ten minutes of research and one minute of speaking.
